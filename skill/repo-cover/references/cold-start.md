# Cold-start evidence inference

Use this route for first-time generation when no existing cover, usable screenshot, runtime capture, brand mark, or suitable repository-owned primary visual can carry the design. Do not assume a previous best cover exists. Absence of visual material permits interpretation, not arbitrary motif selection.

## Build the semantic skeleton

Record four evidence-bound fields before discussing layout or color:

- **Objects:** real entities or data the repository manipulates.
- **Actions:** transformations, detection, organization, lifecycle, or results established by code and documentation.
- **Topology:** sequence, parallel stages, branch, merge, containment, overlap, spatial contact, cycle, or independent collection.
- **Outcome:** user-visible or developer-visible value.

Do not derive the visual from the category alone. `Unity tool`, `math sample`, `network library`, or `AI skill` is not a sufficient motif.

## Choose the representation level

Select one mode and state why it fits:

- **Symbolic abstraction:** mathematical, geometric, algorithmic, sonic, linguistic, or conceptual identity; use notation, fields, rhythm, proportion, and spatial relations.
- **Structural diagram:** workflow, lifecycle, protocol, dependency, or state-transition identity; preserve actual topology.
- **Product-form illustration:** use only when evidence proves a recognizable controller, menu bar, editor, board, instrument, or other form that materially improves identity.
- **Conservative typographic identity:** use when evidence supports a promise but not a defensible visual artifact; prefer type plus one grounded symbol.

Increasing concreteness is not an automatic improvement. Do not replace expressive matrix notation with generic cubes, vibration behavior with a stock controller, or geometric relations with tutorial props unless that product form is the identity.

## Preserve topology

Create a topology ledger that names each important relation and its encoding.

- Keep parallel states separate unless the product actually blends them.
- Keep branches and merges visible.
- Use overlap only for real intersection, masking, compositing, stacking, or contact.
- Use arrows only for evidenced order or lifecycle.
- Keep independent panels adjacent with honest gaps or frames; do not overlap them for decorative depth.

A topology mismatch is a veto failure even when all labels are correct.

## Plan material before palette

Record:

- background and surface lightness relationship;
- warm, cool, neutral, paper, screen, terminal, spatial, or other material character;
- semantic purpose of grid, texture, grain, depth field, or border;
- intended contrast range and focal hierarchy;
- separation method for independent regions.

Pure white and near-black are deliberate materials, not neutral defaults. Do not apply a shared light, dark, glass, gradient, or grid preset without repository-specific reason.

## Classify every asset

Label repository-owned visuals as one of:

- complete artifact;
- brand mark or primary subject;
- component sprite;
- glyph or icon;
- mask or data texture;
- surface texture;
- background;
- weak reference only.

Use assets only within their role. A card background is material, not a finished card; a grayscale mask is transition data, not a scene; an empty capture may prove perspective without being cover-ready.

## Compare directions before rendering

Write at least two compact hypotheses with different representation strategies, not merely palette changes. Compare:

- evidence fidelity;
- abstraction fit;
- topology fidelity;
- aesthetic authorship;
- project specificity;
- thumbnail potential;
- risk of fabricated product form.

Record the chosen direction and why the alternative was weaker. In a batch, reject repeated motifs, panels, palettes, or layout grammar that are not demanded by the repositories.

## Keep inference honest

Create a conceptual proof or visual identity, not a fabricated screenshot or interface. Do not invent translated titles, aliases, feature names, scores, room identifiers, telemetry, status, benchmarks, or product data to make the composition feel complete.

Generic structural labels such as `input`, `mask`, `review`, or `client A` are acceptable only when they describe evidenced roles and cannot be mistaken for captured data.

Reject the design when it jumps from category to stock motif, becomes more literal but more ordinary, flattens real topology, misuses a component as a complete artifact, or depends on an unexplained material preset.
